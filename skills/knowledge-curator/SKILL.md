# Curated knowledge and controlled self-improvement

1. Keep the **personal-only** knowledge library separate from every employer's policy repository. A public standard can be linked across domains only if separately authorized; private employer content never crosses.
2. Watch approved authoritative sources via `bin/ai-source-watch`. A source revision or heading change is an **investigation signal**, not a rule. The approved public-source check runs twice daily (approximately 08:00 and 20:00 Lagos time). A metadata failure is a monitoring failure, not proof nothing changed.
3. Verify the precise statement against the current official source/version and relevant product need. Capture original short interpretation, public URL, publication/review date when known, scope, rights and uncertainty. Do not republish subscription reports, branded screenshots or proprietary design assets.
4. Classify into taxonomy; search existing rules for duplicate/conflicting instructions before proposing additions. Choose `no rule`, `experiment`, `project`, `category` or `global`.
5. Test hypotheses on an authorized personal example. Record actual outcome and negative-scope checks. A lesson cannot become binding based on a single opinion or attractive picture.
6. Use `bin/ai-lessons` to draft an opt-in proposal; require human review, privacy clearance and tests. Approved rule changes go through source-controlled change review. Do not automatically modify the rule registry or push to downstream projects.
7. Retire or amend guidance shown to be misleading, outdated or excessively expensive; retain provenance and rollback. Keep recommendations creative, diverse and not derivative copies.

The curated source catalog contains URLs and **original summaries**, not the authors' protected content. Weekly source checks emit a temporary artifact for review. Reviewers explicitly accept metadata baselines only after verifying reports and never mistake that operation for rule approval.
